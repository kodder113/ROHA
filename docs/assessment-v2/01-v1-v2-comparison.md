# 1. Version 1 versus Version 2 — Item Comparison

All 24 items, grouped by dimension. **13 items are revised and 11 are unchanged.** The structure is unchanged, as approved: six dimensions, four items each, with current and desired ratings on the same 1–5 agreement scale and N/A on the same four items (LE3, OE3, IA2, IA4).

The same data is in [`v1-v2-comparison.csv`](v1-v2-comparison.csv), with estimated reading grades.

**Other Version 2 changes (not item text):**

| Element | Version 1 | Version 2 (proposed) | Reason |
|---|---|---|---|
| Current-state label | Current state — how the organization operates today | Current state — how true this is today | Reads naturally for both first-person items ("My work gives me…") and organizational items |
| Desired-state label | Desired state — how the organization should operate in the future | Desired state — how true this should be in the future | Same; see document 3 on desired-state interpretation |
| OE4 focus label | Clarity of responsibilities and procedures | Clarity of responsibilities | Owner item 4: label matches item |
| Operational Effectiveness definition | "…processes, tools, coordination and clarity enable people to do their work well." | "…processes, tools and technology, coordination between departments, and clarity of responsibilities enable people to do their work well." | Definition matches the four items exactly |
| Strategic Alignment definition | "Whether employees understand organizational priorities and can connect their work to them." | "Whether employees understand the organization's goals, see how their own work connects to them, and hear from leaders what current priorities mean for their team." | Covers the revised SA3 |

**Note on OE4 and procedures.** The brief's fourth Operational Effectiveness topic was "clarity of responsibilities and procedures". A single item covering both would be double-barreled. Version 2 therefore measures **clarity of responsibilities**, and says so in the label and definition. Process quality is already represented by OE1. If Rodrik Consulting wants procedural clarity measured explicitly, that would need a fifth item or a swap for another OE item. Either would be a structural decision, which this draft does not make.

### Operational Effectiveness

| Key | Version 1 (published) | Version 2 (proposed) | Intended construct | Reason for change |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **OE1** | Our work processes allow tasks to be completed without unnecessary steps or delays. | Our work processes let us get things done without unnecessary steps. | Process efficiency: absence of unnecessary steps. | Owner item 3 (one concept). v1 listed two kinds of inefficiency ('steps or delays'). v2 keeps one (unnecessary steps); grade 11.3 → 8.0. |
| **OE2** | I have the tools and technology I need to do my job well. | The tools and technology available to me are well suited to my work. | Fit of available tools and technology to the employee's work. | Owner item 1 (independent expression). v1 followed the 'I have the … I need to do my job …' pattern of a proprietary engagement-survey item. v2 asks whether available tools and technology suit the work, which also captures 'appropriate' from the brief. |
| **OE3** | Departments coordinate effectively when work depends on more than one team. | Departments work well together when a task involves more than one of them. | Coordination between departments on shared work. | Item 5 (consistency). v1 switched between 'departments' and 'team'. v2 uses one referent. |
| **OE4** | It is clear who is responsible for each part of the work I am involved in. | It is clear who is responsible for what in the work I do. | Clarity of responsibilities: knowing who is responsible for what. | Owner item 4 (alignment). The focus label said 'responsibilities and procedures' but the item measured responsibilities only. The label and dimension definition are corrected to 'clarity of responsibilities'; procedures are excluded deliberately to avoid a double-barreled item (see note). The wording is also simplified. |
