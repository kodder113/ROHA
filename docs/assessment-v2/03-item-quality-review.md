| OE | OE1 – OE5 | Efficiency of processes vs. clarity of procedures |
| OE | OE4 – OE5 | Who is responsible vs. how work should be done |
| SI | SI1 – SI2 | Understanding goals vs. one's work advancing them |
| SI | SI4 – SI5 | Ideas are welcomed vs. employees are supported to act on them |

**Across dimensions:** LE2–SI1 (a clear direction supports understanding), OE1–SI3 (efficient organizations may adapt faster) and OC3–SI4 (voice safety supports openness to ideas). These are theoretically expected relationships, not redundancies. The pilot should check that each item loads mainly on its own dimension.

## 3.5 Consistency

- **Referent:** 14 items refer to the respondent ("I", "my", "me": LE1, LE4, LE5, OC3, EE1–EE5, OE2, OE4, OE5, SI1, SI2) and 11 refer to the organization or its people. Each item uses the referent that fits its construct. The composition model for aggregating them into organization-level scores is to be recorded in the methodology document.
- **"Here" / "this organization":** both mean the respondent's organization. Cognitive interviews should confirm that respondents in large organizations do not read "here" as their team.
- **Tense:** present tense throughout, except LE2 ("has set"), which describes an established state.
- **Keying:** all items positively keyed (documented trade-off: acquiescence cannot be detected).

## 3.6 Leading language

No item uses absolute terms ("always", "never"), evaluative adjectives that presume the answer ("excellent", "outstanding"), or assumptions about the respondent. Two items use mildly evaluative verbs appropriate to their constructs: "actually" (OC4), which marks the contrast between stated and enacted values, and "welcomed" (SI4), which is the construct itself. Neither implies a correct answer.
