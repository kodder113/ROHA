# 1. The ROHA Five-Dimension Organizational Health Framework (Version 2 draft)

**Status:** framework approved by the owner on September 26, 2026, subject to licensed intellectual-property screening and the other publication blockers in [document 10](10-publication-readiness-report.md). **Not published.** Version 1 (six dimensions × four items) remains the published assessment.

## 1.1 Structure

| # | Dimension | Code | Items | What it describes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Leadership Effectiveness | LE | 5 | How employees experience senior leadership's integrity, direction-setting, accountability, decision-making and communication of priorities |
| 2 | Organizational Culture | OC | 5 | The quality of everyday working relationships and shared norms: collaboration, equal respect, safety to speak up, learning from mistakes, and consistency between stated values and behavior |
| 3 | Employee Engagement | EE | 5 | The individual's connection to their work and to the organization: meaning, acknowledgement, development, autonomy and commitment |
| 4 | Operational Effectiveness | OE | 5 | The degree to which processes, procedures, tools and technology, coordination between departments, and clarity of responsibilities enable people to do their work well |
| 5 | Strategic Alignment & Innovation | SI | 5 | The organization's capacity to connect people to a shared direction and to renew how it pursues that direction |

**Total: 25 items × 2 perspectives (current and desired) = 50 ratings per respondent**, plus the three unchanged optional open-ended questions. The 1–5 agreement scale and the Not Applicable option (now on LE3, OE3, SI3 and SI5) are unchanged.

### Five aspects per dimension

| Dimension | Aspect 1 | Aspect 2 | Aspect 3 | Aspect 4 | Aspect 5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| LE | Integrity (LE1) | Direction set (LE2) | Accountability consistency (LE3) | Decision confidence (LE4) | Priorities explained (LE5) |

Each aspect is measured by one item and was chosen to be distinguishable from the other four in its dimension. Expected correlations between related aspects are listed in document 3.

## 1.2 Strategic Alignment & Innovation — an integrated construct

The new fifth dimension is **not** the v1 Strategic Alignment and Innovation and Adaptability dimensions placed side by side. It is defined around one idea:

> **Strategic Alignment & Innovation** is the organization's capacity to keep people connected to a shared direction *while* renewing how that direction is pursued. Employees understand where the organization is going and see their work advance it (**alignment**), and the organization adapts effectively when circumstances change and welcomes and enables new ideas (**adaptive innovation**).

**Why direction, adaptability and innovation belong together.** The three are interdependent parts of one organizational capability:

- **Direction gives innovation a purpose.** Without a shared understanding of goals, new ideas and change efforts scatter; energy goes into initiatives that do not move the organization forward.
- **Adaptability keeps direction relevant.** A direction pursued rigidly becomes misaligned with reality when conditions change. Responsiveness is how the organization corrects course without losing its aim.
- **Innovation renews the means.** Openness to ideas and support for employees to test them are how better ways of reaching the goals are discovered, especially by the people closest to the work.

The dimension therefore follows a sequence: **direction known → direction acted on → direction adapted → ideas welcomed → ideas enabled.** A healthy organization scores well across the sequence. Characteristic patterns carry interpretive meaning:

| Pattern | Possible interpretation (hypothesis to investigate, not a finding) |
|---|---|
| High alignment (SI1–SI2), low adaptive innovation (SI3–SI5) | Clear, well-understood direction pursued rigidly; risk of falling behind changing conditions |
| Low alignment, high adaptive innovation | Energetic and open but diffuse; ideas and change may not be connected to shared goals |
| Low across all five | Unclear direction and limited capacity to adapt or improve |

**Boundary with Leadership Effectiveness.** Leadership *behaviors* related to direction (setting it, LE2; explaining priorities, LE5) are measured in Leadership Effectiveness. Strategic Alignment & Innovation measures the *organizational* result: whether direction is understood and acted on, and whether the organization adapts and innovates. Expected correlations such as LE2–SI1 are examined in the pilot (document 3).

**Measurement caveat.** An integrated construct can still behave as two correlated sub-factors (alignment and adaptive innovation). The pilot must test whether one dimension score is justified (document 5). Until then, reports discuss the aspects using item-level results and do not compute sub-scores. Two- and three-item sub-scales would be unreliable.

## 1.3 Why five dimensions of five items

The owner selected a five-dimension structure. The owner has separately developed a five-pillar framework, FLAME; the preference for a small number of broad, integrated dimensions is shared, but ROHA is not derived from FLAME (1.4). From a measurement-design perspective:

- **Five items per dimension** improves on four. It allows more stable internal-consistency estimates and gives a factor model more degrees of freedom. It also restores content the four-item version had to drop (procedural clarity) and adds aspects that were missing (leadership communication, learning from mistakes, autonomy).
- **Five dimensions** removes the separation between Strategic Alignment and Innovation and Adaptability, which v1 treated as independent although they describe linked capabilities (1.2).
- **Survey length** grows from 48 to 50 ratings, which is negligible.
- **Equal weighting** gives each dimension 20% of the overall index (v1: 16.7%).

The structure was chosen for these reasons and at the owner's direction, not to differentiate ROHA from any other instrument.

## 1.4 Independence

**From FLAME.** **FLAME and ROHA are separate frameworks. ROHA is not an implementation, adaptation or version of FLAME** (owner decision, September 26, 2026). ROHA Version 2 was drafted without access to FLAME materials; no FLAME dimension names, definitions, items or scoring rules were used. The only link is that the same owner chose a five-part structure for both. Consequences:

- ROHA materials, reports, the website and the methodology do not mention FLAME, and do not describe ROHA as based on, derived from or aligned with FLAME.
- ROHA's dimension names, definitions, items, scoring and reports are maintained independently of FLAME. A change to one framework does not imply a change to the other.
- If the two are ever mentioned together (for example, in the owner's biography), they are described as separate works by the same author.
- **Owner action (open):** confirm in writing that ROHA's dimension names, definitions and items do not reproduce FLAME content. This confirmation belongs in the authorship record ([prelaunch review, document 7](../prelaunch-review/07-provenance-and-questions.md)).

**From the OCAI.** Version 2 no longer shares the OCAI's six-by-four numeric architecture. That was not the reason for the change (see 1.3). All findings of the prelaunch review about constructs, item form, response format and scoring continue to apply: ROHA measures levels of organizational-health constructs with normative Likert items and has no culture-type classification.

**From other instruments.** The items flagged in the prelaunch review (v1 EE2, EE3, OE2) are rewritten. The three new items were written to avoid known phrasing patterns (document 3). **All 25 items still require screening against licensed copies of relevant instruments before publication.**
